## Course Description
Scientists, engineers, and other technical professionals require skills in computing and data analysis to do their jobs. We refer to these as data science skills.

Examples of data science skills abound. Biologists search thousands of genomes for DNA sequences with special characteristics, such as genes that transcribe non-coding RNA that is “anti-sense” to messenger RNAs. Astronomers search, integrate, and visualize data from many instruments that produce terabytes of complex data. Social scientists do text analytics on massive repositories of social media data to distill patterns in topics and trends in sentiment.

This course teaches graduate students the basic Python programming skills and the use of foundational software tools to do research in data science fields and to be successful technical professionals in the 21st Century.

The course emphasizes a hands-on learning approach in which class time is often used for problem solving in small groups.


## Learning Objectives

Students will learn the following skills:

* Comfort with the Python programming language, including flow control like loops, conditionals, and functions; input and output; and classes
* Understanding of the basics of shell interactions
* How to use text editors such as Vim or Emacs
* How to use Python command line tools
* How to use an IDE such as Visual Studio code
* How to use a Python linter such as Ruff
* How to debug Python programs, including the use of debuggers
* Structuring Python code as modules and files.
* How to use Jupyter notebooks to communicate ideas through Literate Programming and to explore data with the Python scientific stack (numpy, pandas, and matplotlib).


## Grading

The course grade consists of the following components and percentages:

* **Homework (50%)**: Weekly homework grades reflect individual effort. Targets for success are articulated in homework specifications
* **In-class participation (50%)**: Students earn participation grades by completing exercises in small groups during class sessions.

This course is heavily participation-based, and the expectation is that you attend class each week and participate in the in-class activities. However, we do understand that life happpens, and if you are going to miss a class, you must let us know *in advance* whenever possible (eg if you are sick, send an email before class. If you are in an accident and unable to reach your phone, send an email as soon as is reasonable after you are safe). With proper notice, we can arrange for an alternative to in-class participation. **If you are sick, you should not attend class.**

There will be 10 homework assignments with individual scores.
* **Due dates:** To receive full credit, homework must be submitted by 3:59 PM Pacific Time on the due date. The due date for homework is specified in the class syllabus.
  * 20% will be deducted from homework received up to 4 days after its due date. No credit will be given for homework more than 4 days late (unless there is special permission from the instructors). Due to our regrade policy (see below), we are strict with these deadlines and deductions for anything but truly exceptional circumstances.
* **Regrades:** You will have one week from the posting of the assignment grades to revise your homework by submitting your updated homework to the regrade assignment on Gradescope (eg "Homework 1 Regrade"). Regrades are to help with your learning, NOT to aid in procrastination. Use office hours to help you understand your mistakes if you do not already. You may request at most ONE regrade per homework assignment - please make sure you validate your code and understand your assignment's failings before requesting the regrade. Your score will never decrease as a result of requesting a regrade. You may receive up to full credit on a regrade (minus any late penalty).


## Academic Integrity

### Philosophy
*Adapted and extended from Kevin Lin.*

Programming is rarely an individual endeavor, but one that requires significant collaboration in the design, development, testing, evaluation, and deployment of software. Moreover, the emergence of AI code-writing tools is challenging conventional notions about the role of programmers in writing (lots of) code. With that said, our goal in this course is to teach you how to program, not to teach you to use tools that program for you. You still need to learn and practice effectively using core programming 'building blocks'.

To this end, our policies on academic honesty, collaboration, and the use of AI are intended to engender trust and encourage honest and earnest behavior. We want to foster your ability to work with others on challenging problems and to use available tools appropriately. At the same time, this course will challenge you to think independently and programmatically as you learn the core building blocks that you will need in your future computing endeavors.

Our hope is that, when you are struggling, you will take advantage of the resources and policies that we have provided (e.g. consulting course resources, posting on the discussion forum, attending office hours) rather than resorting to dishonest conduct.

### Policy

The policies below apply to all graded work, which includes homework assignments and in-class activities.

You must abide by the following rules in relation to your graded work:

* Do not share solution code with, or view solution code from, any sources, including but not limited to fellow students, prior students, tutors, or the internet.
* Do not provide or receive detailed guidance on, such as being "walked through" or "walking someone through", how to implement any part of a graded assignment to or from anyone who is not a member of the course staff.
* Do not provide or view samples of code that are intended to solve any specific part of a graded assignment’s specification, unless the samples are included in course-provided or course-linked materials.
* Do not use AI in relation to your graded work in a manner not allowed by the course AI policy.
* Do not use forbidden features in graded work, even if you have learned about them from resources that are non-course provided or by working on non-graded work with other people or AI.
* If you choose to utilize a feature that is not forbidden but is not taught in class in your graded work, you should be prepared to explain, in detail, why you chose to use that feature and how it enhances your code beyond what would have been possible using only features taught in class.
    * Being able to explain your code does not guarantee it does not violate this policy, but failing to be able to explain it greatly increases the chances of a violation.

All potential violations of this policy will be reported to the office of Community Standards & Student Conduct for formal investigation. Students found responsible for violations will receive no credit for the assignment(s) on which the policy was violated.

### Using AI in this course

As AI tools become more widely available and more widely used in software development, it is becoming more difficult to a course without acknowledging the existence of these tools. For this reason, we allow the use of AI to help understand core concepts or clarify misconceptions, but emphasize that all work submitted for a grade must be entirely your own.

> *While this policy allows limited use of AI tools, you do not need to use AI to succeed in this course.*

The policy on the use of artificial intelligence (AI) tools is summarized as follows:

> **No part of any graded work may touch an AI tool.**
>
> *You may not copy and paste any work generated by AI into any graded submission, nor may you copy and paste any work from or for a graded assignment into an AI tool. All other uses of AI tools on graded work must be cited.*

* "graded work" means work that will be submitted for a grade, and includes both code and non-code text.
* "copy and paste" includes manually re-typing text.
* "AI tool" means any interface that uses AI, including standalone large language model (LLM) chat platforms (e.g. ChatGPT) and 'built-in' or 'plugin' AI tools (e.g. GitHub Copilot).

The following are examples of allowed and prohibited uses of AI according to this policy:

Allowed
* Asking AI to explain an error message (but not to provide a fix)
* Asking AI to explain the functionality of a provided code snippet that is not part of graded work
* Asking AI to suggest additional information or resources related to a particular topic

Prohibited
* Generating code, comments, or any other work for an assignment
* Using AI to ‘solve’ (part of) an assignment
* Using AI to write, modify, or extend assignments

This policy is grounded in a belief that AI tools can provide value when used responsibly, but that it is also important to first learn the fundamental skills that we are teaching without the use of these tools. There are too many possible uses of AI to enumerate them all, but we have attempted to provide representative examples. When in doubt, please feel free to ask for clarification. If you believe the policy disallows something that should be allowed, please send a message to the instructor.

#### Citing Use of AI

In accordance with the AI policy above, you are required to cite any use of AI in relation to any graded work. The citation must be embedded within your submission as a comment and include the following information:

* The string @cite at the beginning of the citation
* The AI tool and/or model you used (e.g. “GPT-4 on Microsoft Copilot”)
* How you used the AI tool in relation to this assignment

In code files, the AI citation only needs to appear once in the header comment at the top of the file:

```
...
# @cite I used GPT-4 on Microsoft Copilot 
# to explain error messages I encountered while programming.  
...

def hello():
    ...
```

### Productive Collaboration

When you or a friend needs help on graded work, here are some ways you can collaborate that are most likely not in violation of our policies:

* Direct fellow students to specific course-provided or course-linked resources that will help them on graded work
* Explain how course-provided or course-linked examples work, and collaborate with or walk fellow students through non-graded work to help them master high level concepts
* Encourage fellow students to start their assignments early so that there will be time to receive help from course-provided or course-linked resources before the due date

**Extenuating Circumstances: “Don’t Suffer in Silence”**

We recognize that our students come from varied backgrounds and can have widely-varying circumstances. We also acknowledge that the circumstances of this particular quarter may bring unique challenges. If you have any unforeseen circumstances that arise during the course, please do not hesitate to contact the instructor to discuss your situation. The sooner we are made aware, the more easily we can provide accommodations.

Typically, extenuating circumstances include work-school balance, familial responsibilities, health concerns, or anything else beyond your control that may negatively impact your performance in the class. Life happens! While our focus is providing an excellent educational environment, our course does not exist in a vacuum. Our ultimate goal as a course staff is to provide you with the ability to be successful, and we encourage you to work with us to make that happen.
